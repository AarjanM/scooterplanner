# Store and legal compliance for ScooterPlanner

Resolves [#7](https://github.com/AarjanM/scooterplanner/issues/7). Researched 2026-10-21 against primary sources only: Apple developer documentation and App Store Review Guidelines, Google Play Developer Policy Center, Google AdMob/UMP documentation, IAB Europe TCF policies, EDPB guidelines, Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (AP), wetten.overheid.nl, FSF/GNU, and the licence files and release notes of the open-source projects named. Every item gives the requirement, the URL of the rule itself, and the concrete implication for ScooterPlanner. Items marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be confirmed on a primary page and need a second look before the spec relies on them.

Assumptions taken from the map ([#1](https://github.com/AarjanM/scooterplanner/issues/1)): free Android + iOS app from one Expo/React Native codebase, plus a web planner that hands a Route plan to the app by QR code; ads on planning screens only, never during Turn-by-turn navigation, one slot on the web planner; AdMob assumed behind an ad-slot abstraction; one non-consumable remove-ads in-app purchase, restorable per platform, not mirrored across platforms in v1; optional anonymous device account created silently, upgradeable by linking an email (magic link); EU users only; backend on Scaleway (EU); code AGPL-3.0.

Legend: `[ ]` requirement to carry into the spec or build, `[~]` design decision the spec must make, `[!]` unverified.

## 1. Account deletion

Apple and Google both require in-app account deletion once an app "supports account creation". The map already lists in-app deletion as a standing decision; the research below settles what counts as an account and what deletion must cover.

- `[ ]` **Anonymous device account counts as an account for Apple.** Guideline 5.1.1(v): "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app." Apple's FAQ answers the exact ScooterPlanner case: "My app automatically creates an account for the user. Do I need to include an option to initiate account deletion? Yes. People should have the option to delete automatically generated accounts (sometimes called 'guest' accounts) and the data associated with those accounts." Sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage, https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/. Implication: "Delete account" must exist from the first iOS release, before email linking ships, and must wipe the backend record (favourites, saved routes, preferences, device identifiers), not just unlink.
- `[ ]` **Deletion, not deactivation; whole record.** Apple: "Offer to delete the entire account record, along with associated personal data... only offering to temporarily deactivate or disable an account is insufficient." Google: "Temporary account deactivation, disabling, or 'freezing' the app account does not qualify as account deletion." Sources: Apple page above; https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311 (section "Account Deletion Requirement"). Implication: a hard delete (or irreversible anonymisation) of the account row and all data joined to it.
- `[ ]` **Easy to find, in account settings.** Apple: "Make the account deletion option easy to find in your app. Typically, it's included in the app's account settings." Google: "the pathway should be prominent (for example, within the account settings or a similar section)." Customer-service-only flows (phone, email, chat) are not accepted by Apple except for highly regulated apps. Sources: Apple page above; https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111. Implication: Settings → Account → Delete account, one tap away.
- `[ ]` **Confirmation and re-authentication are allowed, friction is not.** Apple: "You may add steps to verify the identity of the person making the request, and to confirm that they want to delete the account, such as by entering a code from an email or phone number already associated with the account", but "apps that make it unnecessarily difficult for someone to delete their account will not pass review." Delay is acceptable if the app states how long it takes and confirms completion; if deletion is scheduled, immediate deletion must also be offered (HIG "Managing accounts", https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts). Implication: one confirmation dialog; for a linked-email account a magic-link re-auth is fine; for an anonymous account no re-auth is possible, so delete immediately on confirmation.
- `[ ]` **Google requires a public web page for deletion requests, declared in Play Console.** "Some users may have already uninstalled your app or not be able to access your in-app experience... your web resource should give users a way to request that their data be deleted." The URL goes in the Data safety form; the page must load, name the app/developer as on Play, and must not send the user back into the app. Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111. Implication: ship `/<site>/delete-account` on the web planner domain before the Android release that includes email linking; it verifies the email by magic link and then deletes. An anonymous account without email cannot be reached from the web (the user knows no identifier), so the in-app path is the only path for it. Optional: show the device-account id in Settings so support can act on it.
- `[!]` **Whether Google's rule covers the silent anonymous account is unverified.** Google defines an app account as "a unique user identity that developers provide as a user-facing feature... generally include a mechanism to verify an identity, such as password, phone number OTP, 2FA, biometric, SSO", and says "accounts that are created and operated offline are not app accounts". A credential-less device record is not addressed. Implication: irrelevant in practice because Apple requires deletion anyway and one codebase ships both; declare "Yes" to the deletion-request question in Data safety from day one.
- `[ ]` **Scope of deletion on Google: everything declared as collected, including at processors.** "Delete data from your own servers and ask service providers to do the same." Retention for "security, fraud prevention or regulatory compliance" is allowed if disclosed in the privacy policy. Source: 13327111. Implication: deletion must also purge backups within the stated retention window (nightly Postgres dumps: state the window, e.g. 30 days) and the privacy policy must say which records survive (e.g. purchase acknowledgement records).
- `[ ]` **Store purchases survive deletion; say so.** Deleting the account must not be presented as a refund path; the remove-ads entitlement lives in the Apple ID / Google account and is restored by the store (section 2). Apple's guidance requires explaining consequences for purchases in the deletion flow. Source: https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/. Implication: deletion dialog copy: "Your store purchase is kept by Apple/Google and can be restored."
- `[ ]` **Offline / signed-out.** Neither store addresses it. Implication: deletion requires connectivity (backend call); when offline, show the web deletion URL and say so.
- `[ ]` **No login wall.** Apple 5.1.1(v) first sentence: "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login." 5.1.1(x): contact information such as email may be requested only if "optional for the user, features and services are not conditional on providing the information". Guideline 4.8 (Sign in with Apple) does not apply to apps that "exclusively use the company's own account system". Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage. Implication: routing and navigation work with no account prompt ever; email linking stays optional and gates nothing except sync; no Sign in with Apple needed.

## 2. In-app purchase (non-consumable remove-ads)

- `[ ]` **Must be a store IAP on both platforms.** Apple 3.1.1: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app... you must use in-app purchase. Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys, augmented reality markers, QR codes". Google Payments policy §2: apps "requiring or accepting payment for access to in-app features or services" must use Google Play's billing system. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#payments, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738. Implication: StoreKit 2 non-consumable on iOS, Play Billing one-time product (`ProductType.INAPP`, non-consumable) on Android. The QR handoff must never carry an entitlement.
- `[ ]` **Restore mechanism required on iOS; Google needs no button but needs a query.** Apple 3.1.1: "make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases." StoreKit 2: "Include some mechanism in your app, such as a Restore Purchases button... In rare cases... call AppStore.sync()", which "displays a system prompt that asks users to authenticate... Call this function only in response to an explicit user action"; on launch read `Transaction.currentEntitlements` (refunded/revoked products do not appear). Google: "you must call BillingClient.queryPurchasesAsync() to ensure your app processes all purchases" on every billing connection; "A user may buy an item on one device and then expect to see the item when they switch devices." Sources: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/appstore/sync(), https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction/currententitlements, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/restoring-purchased-products, https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate. Implication: iOS: entitlement from `currentEntitlements` at launch plus a "Restore purchases" button in Settings that calls `AppStore.sync()`; Android: `queryPurchasesAsync()` at every billing connection, optional restore button. `[!]` That a visible Restore button is strictly mandatory for approval is reviewer practice, not policy text.
- `[ ]` **Acknowledge within three days on Android.** Acknowledgement "must be done within three days so that the purchase isn't automatically refunded and entitlement revoked"; acknowledge only in `PURCHASED` state; check `isAcknowledged()` first; server-side `Purchases.products:acknowledge` is recommended. Source: https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate#process. Implication: acknowledge from the backend (also gives a server-side entitlement record) or from the client if the backend is unreachable.
- `[ ]` **Hide the buy button once owned.** Google: "Before offering an item for sale, check that the user does not already own the item." Source: https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/one-time-products. Implication: ad-slot abstraction consults the entitlement before rendering the remove-ads offer.
- `[ ]` **Handle refunds and revocations.** iOS: product disappears from `currentEntitlements` / gets `revocationDate`; Android: Real-time Developer Notifications or `Purchases.products` state. Implication: ads return on refund; cache the entitlement offline with a TTL rather than forever.
- `[ ]` **No in-app steering to another payment method; outside-the-app talk is fine.** Apple 3.1.1(a): outside the US storefront "apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase." 3.1.3: "Developers can send communications outside of the app to their user base about purchasing methods other than in-app purchase." Google §4: no leading users "to a payment method other than Google Play's billing system" via the listing, in-app promotions, webviews, buttons, links, messaging or sign-up flows; communications outside the app are not addressed. Sources as above. Implication: the web planner may say "the app has a one-time remove-ads purchase"; the apps and their store listings must never link to or mention a web checkout.
- `[~]` **Honouring a web-bought ad-free flag.** Apple 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform Services: apps "may allow users to access content, subscriptions, or features they have acquired... on other platforms or your web site... provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app." Google has no explicit equivalent `[!]`. Implication: v1 has no web purchase (out of scope per the map), so nothing to do; if a cross-platform mirror comes later, keep the store IAP and honour either entitlement (OR), and keep the Android app silent about the web purchase.
- `[~]` **EU/DMA alternative payment terms are not worth it for a low-price one-off.** Apple's EU terms (ADPLA Attachment 14, effective 2026-10-01) allow alternative processors and out-of-app offers but require the "StoreKit External Purchases or Offers Entitlement", iOS 26.2+, a system disclosure sheet, IAP shown "at least as prominently", a 12-month lock-in, and commissions still apply. Google's EEA alternative billing, user-choice billing and external-offers programmes all require a registered business and API integration; external offers forbid Play Billing in the same app. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/support/dma-and-apps-in-the-eu/, https://developer.apple.com/support/payment-options-on-the-app-store-in-the-eu/, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12348241, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13821247, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14372887. `[!]` Apple's EU page is internally inconsistent on whether a 15% "store services commission" survives; check Attachment 14 if this is ever pursued. Implication: standard IAP; Apple Small Business Program (15%) and Google's 15% tier under $1M apply.
- `[~]` **Family Sharing (iOS only).** Non-consumables may be Family-Shared; "After you enable Family Sharing for an Apple In-App Purchase, you can't turn it off." Google: "You can't share in-app purchases... with your family members." Sources: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/supporting-family-sharing-in-your-app, https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7007852. Implication: decide before launch; irreversible; creates an iOS/Android asymmetry. Default suggestion: off.
- `[ ]` **Prices are VAT-inclusive, localised, never hard-coded.** Apple generated prices "account for foreign exchange rates and certain taxes"; Google is "responsible for determining, charging, and remitting VAT" and prices shown must equal the amount paid; Google §6: in-app price must match the Play billing UI. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/set-a-price-for-an-in-app-purchase/, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000. Implication: render `Product.displayPrice` / `getFormattedPrice()`; copy says "one-time purchase, removes ads on planning screens".
- `[ ]` **EU 14-day withdrawal right is handled by the stores.** Both make the consumer agree to immediate delivery of digital content, waiving withdrawal (Apple NL terms "Herroepingsrecht"; Google: "you waive your automatic statutory right of withdrawal"). Apple refunds are discretionary via reportaproblem.apple.com; Google refunds within 48 h via Google, after that "Contact the developer". Sources: https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/nl/terms.html, https://support.apple.com/en-us/118223, https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/15576634. Implication: publish a refund contact (Android post-48h requests land on the developer) and a refund policy line in the store listing.

## 3. Ad consent and privacy disclosures

### 3.1 Consent flow in the apps (AdMob / UMP / TCF)

- `[ ]` **Google's EU User Consent Policy puts the consent duty on ScooterPlanner.** For EEA/UK/CH users the publisher must obtain consent for "the use of cookies or other local storage where legally required" and for "the collection, sharing, and use of personal data for personalization of ads", "retain records of consent", "provide clear instructions for withdrawing consent", and "clearly identify each party that may use end users' personal data". Source: https://www.google.com/about/company/user-consent-policy/. Implication: consent is collected before the first ad request, recorded (the TC string), withdrawable in-app, and the privacy policy names Google and every mediation partner.
- `[ ]` **A Google-certified, TCF-integrated CMP is mandatory for personalised ads in the EEA.** "a certified CMP integrated with the TCF is required when serving personalized ads to users in the EEA and UK" (since 2024-01-16); non-certified traffic is "eligible for non-personalized ads or limited ads". Google's own "Google LLC CMP" (TCF CMP ID 300) is on the certified list, and "The Google UMP SDK is IAB-certified for TCF v2.3 across all SDK versions." TCF v2.3 "is mandatory for all TC strings generated on or after March 1, 2026." Sources: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/13554116, https://support.google.com/admob/answer/10113207, https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9999955. Implication: AdMob "Privacy & messaging" GDPR message + UMP SDK, no third-party CMP. If the ad network is swapped (Ad Alliance, #8), the replacement must consume the same TC string or a CMP from the certified list must replace UMP; this is a constraint on the ad-slot abstraction (#11).
- `[ ]` **UMP integration contract.** Call `requestConsentInfoUpdate()` on every launch, then `loadAndShowConsentFormIfRequired()` / `loadAndPresentIfRequired(from:)`; gate every ad load behind `canRequestAds()`; do not read consent from an app cache; when `privacyOptionsRequirementStatus == REQUIRED`, "add a visible and interactable UI element to your app that presents the privacy options form"; remove `ConsentDebugSettings` before release. Sources: https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy, https://developers.google.com/admob/ios/privacy. Implication: a "Privacy and ad settings" row in Settings that opens the privacy-options form; this is also what satisfies TCF Policy C(f) and GDPR Art. 7(3) (withdrawal as easy as giving). Expo: use a maintained wrapper (e.g. react-native-google-mobile-ads) that exposes UMP; verify it in #4/#11.
- `[ ]` **What serves when the user declines.** Google serves personalised ads only with consent for TCF Purposes 1, 3, 4 (and LI or consent for 2, 7, 9, 10). Without 3/4 but with Purpose 1: non-personalised ads, which "do still use cookies or mobile ad identifiers for frequency capping and aggregated ad reporting" so still need Purpose 1 consent under ePrivacy. Without Purpose 1: "limited ads", which "disable the collection, sharing, and use of personal data for personalization of ads", no ad ID, no frequency capping, contextual and IP-level general location only; "Programmatic limited ads are turned on by default". Sources: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9760862, https://support.google.com/admob/answer/7676680, https://support.google.com/admob/answer/10105530, https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure. Implication: a refusing user still sees (limited) ads and the full app; nothing extra to code, the SDK reads the TC string. Do not use the legacy `npa` request extra as a substitute. `[!]` Exact "Limited ads 2.0" purpose table (31 March 2025) seen only in search snippets.
- `[ ]` **TCF publisher duties discharged by Google's message, with four leftovers.** TCF 2.2+: consent is the only lawful basis for Purposes 3–6 (no LI for personalisation); first layer must state the number of vendors; users must be able to "withdraw their consent as easily as it was to give it" and "resurface the Framework UI from an easily accessible link"; granular choices default off; a public privacy policy with the legally required disclosures and a TCF compliance attestation naming the CMP ID. Sources: https://iabeurope.eu/tcf-2-2/, https://iabeurope.eu/iab-europe-transparency-consent-framework-policies/. Implication: (i) privacy-options entry point, (ii) privacy-policy URL in the AdMob message, (iii) keep the ad-partner list in AdMob minimal, (iv) a line in the privacy policy "ScooterPlanner participates in the IAB Europe TCF and uses CMP ID 300 (Google)".
- `[ ]` **Enable the "Do not consent" button on the first layer.** AdMob's message editor offers an optional single-click "Do not consent" on the first layer; the AP's banner rules require reject and accept on the same layer, no pre-ticked boxes, no inconspicuous reject link, and "legitimate interest is not a valid legal basis for displaying personalised advertisements". Sources: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/10113207, https://autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/themes/internet-and-smart-devices/cookies/clear-cookie-banners. Implication: turn it on; the Dutch regulator actively audits banners (AP campaigns Nov 2025, Oct 2026 on its cookies page).
- `[ ]` **iOS: App Tracking Transparency on top of TCF consent.** Apple: "Placing a third-party SDK in your app that combines user data from your app with user data from other developers' apps to target advertising... even if you don't use the SDK for these purposes" is tracking and needs ATT permission; denied means the IDFA "will be all zeros and you may not track them"; 5.1.2(i) "You must receive explicit permission from users via the App Tracking Transparency APIs to track their activity"; apps "may not require users to enable system functionalities (such as push notifications, location services, tracking) in order to access functionality". AdMob: add `NSUserTrackingUsageDescription`, call `requestTrackingAuthorization`, wait for the callback before loading ads, add `SKAdNetworkItems` (Google's `cstr6suwn9.skadnetwork` plus mediation partners); Google notes the EU consent policy "still applies" alongside ATT. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/, https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage, https://developers.google.com/admob/ios/ios14, https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9997589. Implication: order on iOS: UMP GDPR message → (if consented) optional IDFA explainer → ATT prompt → load ads. GDPR consent does not substitute for ATT. `[!]` Google's exact serving behaviour on ATT-denied is not stated on a Google page; inferred from Apple (IDFA zeroed → no IDFA-based personalisation).
- `[~]` **Simplest no-tracking posture: never request ATT, serve limited/NPA only.** The map's "no-tracking posture" and the Ad Alliance alternative suggest a design choice: skipping ATT means the IDFA is always zero on iOS and the Apple label can avoid "Data Used to Track You". Trade-off is revenue. Decide in #11.
- `[ ]` **Remove-ads is not a "consent or pay" wall, provided refusing consent costs nothing.** EDPB Guidelines 05/2020: "access to services and functionalities must not be made conditional on the consent of a user to the storing of information... (so called cookie walls)"; withdrawal must be possible "without detriment" and "without lowering service levels"; Example 8 (an app that "now only works to a limited extent" after revoking consent) invalidates consent, while reverting to non-personalised offers is not detriment. EDPB Opinion 08/2024 is "limited to... large online platforms" and recommends a "Free Alternative Without Behavioural Advertising... e.g. contextual or general advertising". AP: a cookie wall where "you do not have access to the website or app if you refuse the tracking cookies" is "prohibited under the GDPR". Sources: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf (paras 38–41, 46–50, 113–115), https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-04/edpb_opinion_202408_consentorpay_en.pdf (paras 22–31, 73–75), https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/themes/internet-and-smart-devices/cookies. Implication: routing, navigation and sync are identical for consenting, refusing and paying users; the only variable is ad presence and ad type. Never present "pay to remove ads" as the way to avoid tracking; refusing is free.
- `[ ]` **Legitimate interest is not available for personalised ads; ePrivacy needs consent for device access anyway.** Telecommunicatiewet art. 11.7a(1): storing or accessing information on the user's terminal equipment is allowed only if the user "is voorzien van duidelijke en volledige informatie... en daarvoor toestemming heeft verleend", except for communication, "strikt noodzakelijk" delivery of the requested service, or quality/effectiveness measurement with "geen of geringe gevolgen" for privacy (11.7a(3)). The CJEU in KNLTB (C-621/22, 2024) held a purely commercial interest can be a legitimate interest "only under strict conditions" (AP summary); the AP's banner page keeps "legitimate interest is not a valid legal basis for displaying personalised advertisements". Sources: https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0009950/2025-06-28 (art. 11.7a), https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/actueel/ap-en-knltb-beeindigen-zaak-rond-boete, AP banner page above. `[!]` CJEU wording taken from the EUR-Lex index and the AP summary, not the judgment page. Implication: ad ID and ad-SDK storage are consent-only (TCF Purpose 1); the app's own analytics/crash reporting is either strictly necessary + low-impact (allowed without consent, must be disclosed) or added as a publisher purpose in the AdMob message (https://support.google.com/admob/answer/10305345).
- `[ ]` **Do not join the account id with the advertising id.** Google Play Advertising ID policy: the ad ID "may not be connected to persistent device identifiers" and "may only be connected to personally-identifiable information with the explicit consent of the user"; for analytics it "may not be connected to personally-identifiable information or associated with any persistent device identifier"; Android 13+ apps must declare `com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID` (the Mobile Ads SDK merges it); "For essential non ads use-cases such as analytics and fraud prevention, use App Set ID." Sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6048248. Implication: no custom targeting or user-id in ad requests; no server-side join of ad events and account id; the ad ID stays inside the SDK. A no-ads build (e.g. F-Droid) must strip the AD_ID permission.

### 3.2 Web planner ad slot (ePrivacy on the web)

- `[ ]` **The single web ad slot needs the same consent regime.** Tw art. 11.7a applies to any storage/access on terminal equipment via the web too; the AP requires, before consent, information on which cookies are used, who processes data, which data and for which purpose, and the right to withdraw, "voordat u toestemming geeft of weigert"; scrolling or continuing is not consent; a cookie wall is "onder de AVG verboden". Tracking-cookie lifetime of "een half jaar of langer is snel te lang". Sources: https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0009950/2025-06-28, https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/themas/internet-slimme-apparaten/cookies, https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/themas/internet-slimme-apparaten/cookies/trackingcookies. Implication: a TCF-registered CMP on the web planner (Google's web "European regulations message" is the same CMP ID 300 if AdSense/Ad Manager is used; an Ad Alliance web tag needs its own certified CMP); no ad tag loads before consent; the planner itself (routing, QR) must work with everything refused; prefer no analytics cookies, or "beperkt analytische cookies" only.
- `[ ]` **Functional storage is exempt but must be disclosed.** AP: functional cookies (login, language) need no consent but "de eigenaar [moet] u vertellen dat de cookies worden geplaatst", e.g. in the privacy statement. Implication: the QR/route-id handoff and language preference can use local storage without a banner, listed in the privacy policy.

### 3.3 Privacy policy content

- `[ ]` **One public HTML privacy policy (NL + EN), linked from both store listings and inside both apps.** Apple 5.1.1(i): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner", and it must "Identify what data... the app/service collects, how it collects that data, and all uses", confirm third parties such as "advertising networks and third-party SDKs" give equal protection, and "Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion". Google: "All apps must post a privacy policy link in the designated field within Play Console" and in the app, on "an active, publicly accessible and non-geofenced URL (no PDFs)", labelled as a privacy policy, with "Developer information and a privacy point of contact". Sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311.
- `[ ]` **GDPR Art. 13 contents (AP checklist):** controller identity and contact; DPO if any; purposes and legal basis per processing; recipients or categories (name Google LLC/AdMob and each mediation partner, Scaleway as processor, the email provider for magic links); transfers outside the EEA and their ground (Google under SCCs); retention periods per data class (account data, saved routes, server logs, backups, purchase acknowledgements); data-subject rights; right to withdraw consent at any time; right to complain to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; whether provision is mandatory and consequences; automated decision-making (none). Source: https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/themas/basis-avg/privacyrechten-avg/recht-op-informatie. `[!]` Art. 13 verbatim not fetched (EUR-Lex blocked); the AP list is the regulator's rendering of it. Implication: also state the TCF participation and CMP ID, the limited-ads fallback, that remove-ads is optional and not the way to refuse tracking, the account-deletion URL, and that EU hosting is on Scaleway.
- `[ ]` **Consent-specific disclosures (EDPB 05/2020 para 64):** controller identity, purpose of each processing operation, data collected, right to withdraw, and all controllers relying on the consent must be named. Implication: covered by the AdMob message's vendor list plus the policy.

### 3.4 Apple App Privacy label

Apple: "You need to identify all of the data you or your third-party partners collect"; "If your app uses third-party code, such as advertising or analytics SDKs, you must describe what data it collects, how it is used, and whether it tracks users." Collect = "transmitting data off the device in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it for a period longer than what is necessary to service the transmitted request in real time." Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/. AdMob's own list of what its iOS SDK collects: IP address, crash logs, performance data, device ID (advertising identifier), advertising data, product interactions. Source: https://developers.google.com/admob/ios/privacy/data-disclosure. Build the label from Xcode's Product → Archive → Generate Privacy Report (aggregates SDK privacy manifests); the Mobile Ads SDK ships a manifest since 11.2.0, UMP since 2.3.0. Source: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files.

- `[ ]` Expected declarations (Linked / Tracking are our mapping, `[!]` Google does not publish Apple-label flags):
  - Precise Location — App Functionality; linked if positions or routes are stored against the account id; declare only if GPS leaves the device (server-side routing does send origin/destination/position).
  - Coarse Location — Third-Party Advertising (AdMob IP-derived location; `[!]` closest fit since Apple has no "IP address" type).
  - Email Address — App Functionality / account; linked; only for linked accounts.
  - User ID (anonymous account id) — App Functionality; linked.
  - Device ID (IDFA via AdMob) — Third-Party Advertising; linked; Tracking = yes if ATT is requested and personalised ads are served, otherwise follow the Xcode report.
  - Advertising Data, Product Interaction — Third-Party Advertising / Analytics.
  - Crash Data, Performance Data, Other Diagnostic Data — Analytics / App Functionality (AdMob plus any crash reporter).
  - Search History — only if address/route searches are retained server-side.
  - Purchase History — only if entitlement state is stored on the backend.
- `[ ]` **Privacy manifest and signed SDKs.** Apps must include a `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`; listed third-party SDKs (Firebase*, GoogleUtilities, etc.) need their own manifest and signature. Source: https://developer.apple.com/support/third-party-SDK-requirements/. `[!]` Required-reason API categories page not fetched (JS-only). Implication: Expo manages the manifest; audit the report before each submission.

### 3.5 Google Play Data safety form

Google: data "collected and handled through any third-party libraries or SDKs" must be declared; advertising ID goes under "Device or other identifiers"; declare encryption in transit, deletion-request mechanism, optional vs required, and purposes; "The developer is responsible for the accuracy of the label." Sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469, https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure (AdMob SDK collects IP address, product interactions, diagnostics, ad ID and App Set ID; all over TLS).

- `[ ]` Expected entries: Location › Approximate (AdMob, shared, Advertising) and Precise (collected, App functionality, required, if sent to the backend); Personal info › Email address (collected, optional, Account management) and User IDs (collected, App functionality); App activity › App interactions (shared, Advertising/Analytics) and In-app search history (if stored); App info and performance › Crash logs, Diagnostics; Device or other IDs (shared, Advertising/Analytics/Fraud prevention); Financial info › Purchase history only if stored on the backend. All encrypted in transit; "Yes" to deletion requests; account-deletion URL filled in.
- `[ ]` **Target audience: 16–17 and 18+, not Families.** Dutch AM licence is from 16; selecting any group including children triggers Families policy and self-certified ads SDKs; the GDPR/UAVG age of consent in NL is 16. Sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159, https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6219315. Implication: no child-directed ad tagging; keep listing copy free of child appeal; Apple age rating consistent.

## 4. Location permissions and background execution

Headline: Turn-by-turn navigation needs neither iOS "Always" nor Android `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION`. Both platforms let a While-in-use app keep receiving location in the background as long as the session was started in the foreground and is visibly indicated.

### 4.1 iOS

- `[ ]` **Request location only when needed, explain it, offer a fallback.** Guideline 5.1.5: "Use Location Services in your app only when it is directly relevant to the features and services provided by the app... Ensure that you notify and obtain consent before collecting, transmitting, or using location data. If your app uses Location Services, be sure to explain the purpose in your app." 5.1.1(iv): "if a user declines to share Location, offer the ability to manually enter an address." 5.1.1(iii): request only data "relevant to the core functionality". Core Location: "make authorization requests only when someone engages a part of your app that requires that data"; requesting at launch risks denial. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/requesting-authorization-to-use-location-services. Implication: prompt on first "use my location" or "Start navigation", never at launch; Route plan works with typed addresses when denied. `[!]` The oft-quoted "Apps that use background location must clearly disclose..." sentence is not in the current guidelines.
- `[ ]` **"When In Use" is enough for background navigation.** "If you enable background location updates, an app with When in Use authorization continues to run in the background when location services are active"; "Always authorization... Request this access level only when necessary." Mechanism: `UIBackgroundModes` = `location` plus `allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates = true` set "while the app is in the foreground" ("Setting the value to true but omitting the UIBackgroundModes key... is a fatal error"), or on iOS 17+ a `CLBackgroundActivitySession` ("allows a when-in-use authorized app to receive location updates"), created in the foreground. Apple lists "Provide navigation instructions in real time" as a legitimate reason for background updates. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/handling-location-updates-in-the-background, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/allowsbackgroundlocationupdates, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clbackgroundactivitysession-3mzv3. Implication: never request Always; start the session on "Start navigation" with the app visible; end it on arrival/cancel. Handle "Allow Once" (navigation stops when the app is no longer in use). The blue status pill shows automatically for When-In-Use background use (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/showsbackgroundlocationindicator); that is Apple's intended transparency, not a defect.
- `[ ]` **Background modes only for their purpose.** 2.5.4: "Multitasking apps may only use background services for their intended purposes: VoIP, audio playback, location, task completion, local notifications, etc." Xcode modes table: `audio` = "The app plays audible content in the background"; `location` = "provides location-based information and requires the use of the platform's standard location service"; "Use background execution modes sparingly". Sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#software-requirements, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes. Implication: `location` is what keeps navigation alive; add `audio` only if spoken instructions must play while locked, with an `AVAudioSession` (`.playback`, ducking others) active only while a prompt plays (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/configuring-your-app-for-media-playback). `[!]` Apple does not address intermittent TTS explicitly; do not rely on `audio` alone as a keep-alive.
- `[ ]` **Usage strings.** `NSLocationWhenInUseUsageDescription` is required ("Authorization requests fail immediately if the required keys aren't present"); `NSLocationAlwaysAndWhenInUseUsageDescription` only if Always were requested. Source: Core Location authorisation page above. Implication: NL + EN strings naming the feature ("to show your position and guide you along a Legal route").
- `[ ]` **Reduced accuracy (iOS 14+).** Users may grant approximate location; add `NSLocationTemporaryUsageDescriptionDictionary` and call `requestTemporaryFullAccuracyAuthorization(withPurposeKey:)` (or a `CLServiceSession` with `fullAccuracyPurposeKey`, which re-asks automatically). Sources: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/nslocationtemporaryusagedescriptiondictionary, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/requesttemporaryfullaccuracyauthorization(withpurposekey:). Implication: Route plan works with approximate location; Turn-by-turn navigation requests precise on start and explains why it cannot run otherwise.
- `[ ]` **App Review notes and demo.** "If features require an environment that is hard to replicate... be prepared to provide a demo video"; missing details "may delay" or fail review. Source: https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/. Implication: supply a short video of background navigation and GPX-simulation instructions in Review Notes. `[!]` No Apple page states a demo video is mandatory for background location specifically.

### 4.2 Android

- `[ ]` **Foreground service of type `location`.** Android 14+ (targetSdk 34): "you must declare an appropriate service type for each foreground service" and "request the appropriate foreground service permission for that type". For `location`: manifest `android:foregroundServiceType="location"`, permission `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION`, and the user must have granted `ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` or `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`; described as "Long-running use cases that require location access, such as navigation". "you cannot create a location foreground service while your app is in the background, unless you've been granted the ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION runtime permission" (SecurityException). Sources: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fg-service-types, https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start. Implication: start the service from the visible navigation screen; stop it the moment navigation ends. Spoken instructions can be issued from that service; `mediaPlayback` type is not needed for TTS `[!]` (no primary text ties TTS to it; avoid declaring it).
- `[ ]` **No `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION`.** Android: "The system considers your app to be using foreground location if... Your app is running a foreground service... Your app retains access when it's placed in the background, such as when the user presses the Home button or turns their device's display off." Background permission is for features that "constantly share location with other users or use the Geofencing API". Play policy lists "Turn-by-turn navigation, unless it runs while the user is outside the app (for example, passive route tracking...)" among features that "could use foreground access instead", and "we may reject apps that request or access background location without compelling justification". Sources: https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170. Implication: do not declare the permission; this also avoids the Play background-location declaration, prominent-disclosure text and video.
- `[ ]` **Play's foreground-service conditions.** FGS location use is permitted when it "has been initiated as a continuation of an in-app, user-initiated action" and "is terminated immediately after the intended use case of the user-initiated action is completed by the application"; if FGS use becomes equivalent to background location, background rules apply. Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170. Implication: no passive tracking after arrival; no "keep tracking in case you ride again".
- `[ ]` **Play Console foreground-service declaration with video.** Apps targeting Android 14+ "need to declare any foreground service types that you use" with description, user impact, use case, and a video that "should demonstrate the steps the user needs to take in your app in order to trigger the feature"; "All foreground service types are subject to review." Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821. Implication: one declaration for TYPE_LOCATION; record a ≤30 s clip (start navigation → notification → home/screen off → guidance continues).
- `[ ]` **Request fine and coarse together; work with approximate.** "don't request the ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION permission by itself... request both... in a single runtime request"; "Your app should still work when the user grants only approximate location access"; users may pick "Approximate" on Android 12+, with an upgrade flow. Sources: https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions/runtime, https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions. Implication: same behaviour as iOS: planning works approximate, navigation asks to upgrade to precise.
- `[ ]` **Minimum scope, no location for ads.** "Apps should request the minimum scope necessary"; "You should never request location permissions from users for the sole purpose of advertising or analytics"; apps "may not access data protected by location permissions... after it is no longer necessary". Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170. Implication: the ad-slot abstraction must not pass device location to the ad SDK (AdMob derives coarse location from IP on its own); ads are off during navigation anyway.
- `[ ]` **Honest persistent notification.** The FGS notification is what makes background location "foreground" in Android's model. Implication: "Navigating to … — tap to return", with a Stop action.

### 4.3 CarPlay / Android Auto (out of scope, for the record)

CarPlay navigation requires the `com.apple.developer.carplay-maps` entitlement, Apple's CarPlay Entitlement Addendum and extra review guidelines (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/carplay/requesting-carplay-entitlements); Android Auto navigation requires the `androidx.car.app.category.NAVIGATION` category and `onAutoDriveEnabled` for review (https://developer.android.com/training/cars/apps/navigation). Mopeds have no car head unit; skip.

## 5. Navigation disclaimers

No store rule mandates a traffic-sign disclaimer; the industry uses two patterns, and Dutch law puts the duty to obey signs on the rider, which is why the disclaimer matters for a "Legal route" product.

- `[ ]` **What the big apps say (ToS pattern).** Google Maps Additional Terms §3 "Actual Conditions; Assumption of Risk": "you may find that actual conditions differ from the map results and content, so exercise your independent judgment and use Google Maps at your own risk. You're responsible at all times for your conduct and its consequences" (https://www.google.com/help/terms_maps/); Google Maps Help: "When in doubt, follow actual traffic regulations and confirm signage from the road or path that you're on" (https://support.google.com/maps/answer/144339). Waze Terms: "you are responsible for complying with local traffic laws and regulations"; "The information provided by the Service is not intended to replace the information presented on the road"; where a sign "instructs differently than the Service, you must not rely on the Service"; "You must not attempt to view or click on any ads while operating a vehicle" (https://support.google.com/waze/answer/12373727). Apple Maps Terms §4.5: "FOR YOUR SAFETY, USE YOUR INDEPENDENT JUDGMENT AND ALWAYS PAY ATTENTION TO POSTED SIGNS AND CURRENT ROUTE CONDITIONS. FOLLOW SAFE PRACTICES AND LOCAL TRAFFIC REGULATIONS" (https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/maps/terms-en.html); iOS SLA adds that location data "is provided for basic navigational and planning purposes only" (https://www.apple.com/legal/sla/docs/iOS26_iPadOS26.pdf). All ToS-only, no in-app tap. `[!]` Whether Google Maps shows a first-navigation safety banner in-app was not verified.
- `[ ]` **In-app Accept dialog (Organic Maps pattern).** Before the first route, Organic Maps shows "When following the route, please keep in mind: — Road conditions, traffic laws, and road signs always take priority over navigation hints; — The map might be inaccurate, and the suggested route might not always be the most optimal way to reach the destination; — Suggested routes should only be understood as recommendations; ... Please stay alert and safe on the roads!" with Accept/Decline; routing starts only on Accept. Sources: https://github.com/organicmaps/organicmaps/blob/master/data/strings/strings.txt (`dialog_routing_disclaimer_*`), https://github.com/organicmaps/organicmaps/blob/master/iphone/Maps/UI/ReusableComponents/CustomAlerts/AlertController/MWMAlertViewController.mm. OsmAnd has no routing disclaimer string, only a speed-camera legality dialog and ToS "The entire risk as to the results and performance of the Software... is assumed by you" (https://github.com/osmandapp/OsmAnd/blob/master/OsmAnd/res/values/strings.xml, https://osmand.net/docs/legal/terms-of-use/). `[!]` Organic Maps Android implementation not located.
- `[ ]` **Store rules: none require it, but do not encourage unsafe riding.** Apple 1.4.4: apps "should never encourage drunk driving or other reckless behavior such as excessive speed"; 1.4.5: apps "should not urge customers to participate in activities... that risks physical harm"; 5.1.5: no "autonomous control over vehicles". Google Play: no disclaimer rule in the location/permission policies read; driver-distraction design rules apply only to Android Auto. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#physical-harm, https://developer.android.com/training/cars/apps. Implication: no ETA gamification, no speed-camera or police-check features (already out of scope).
- `[ ]` **Dutch law puts the duty on the rider.** RVV 1990 art. 62: "Weggebruikers zijn verplicht gevolg te geven aan de verkeerstekens die een gebod of verbod inhouden"; art. 63: "Verkeerstekens gaan boven verkeersregels, voor zover deze regels onverenigbaar zijn met deze tekens"; art. 5 and 6 set where Fiets, Snorfiets and Bromfiets ride. Source: https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825/. Implication: a Legal route is a recommendation computed from map data for the selected Vehicle class; the sign on the road wins. `[!]` Bijlage 1 (sign definitions G11/G12a/G13) was not reached in the fetched text; #9 should cite it.
- `[~]` **Recommended for ScooterPlanner.** One-time in-app dialog before the first Turn-by-turn navigation, Organic Maps style, NL + EN, with explicit Accept, saying: traffic signs and the RVV take priority over instructions; the route is a recommendation computed for the Vehicle class you selected, so select the right one (Bromfiets, Snorfiets, Speed pedelec counts as Bromfiets); map data may be wrong or outdated; do not operate the phone while riding. Repeat in the Terms. Show the selected Vehicle class permanently on the navigation screen. Add a "report wrong road type" affordance so misclassified G11/G12a segments can be corrected (ties to #3/#9).

## 6. AGPL-3.0 in the App Store

- `[ ]` **The tension is Apple's Usage Rules versus "no further restrictions".** FSF (2010, GNU Go): Apple's Terms impose restrictions (then a five-device limit) that GPL §6 forbids: "You may not impose any further restrictions on the recipients' exercise of the rights granted herein"; "This is the license condition that Apple is violating when it distributes GPL-covered software through the App Store"; "the Usage Rules are imposed on you no matter how the software is licensed"; the analysis "would apply to all versions of the GNU GPL and AGPL". Sources: https://www.fsf.org/news/2010-05-app-store-compliance, https://www.fsf.org/blogs/licensing/more-about-the-app-store-gpl-enforcement. Today's Apple Media Services Terms still say "You may use the Services and Content only for personal, noncommercial purposes", cap signed-in devices at ten, and license apps as "licensed, not sold" under a "nontransferable license" unless a Custom EULA overrides (https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/). Implication: distributing unmodified AGPL code through the App Store is a licence conflict that only the copyright holders can object to. Apple did not remove GNU Go on its own initiative; it reacted to the FSF as copyright-holder representative. `[!]` "No enforcement since 2011" is the absence of a rule, not a documented policy; the Review Guidelines contain no rule on GPL/open source.
- `[ ]` **AGPL §13 is a server duty, not a client blocker.** §13: "if you modify the Program, your modified version must prominently offer all users interacting with it remotely through a computer network... an opportunity to receive the Corresponding Source". GNU FAQ: a program is covered if "expressly designed to accept user requests and send responses over a network". Sources: https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html, https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#AGPLv3InteractingRemotely. Implication: the mobile client sends requests, it does not serve them; §13 bites whoever runs a modified ScooterPlanner backend (which is the intended effect for the routing rules). The backend must expose a "source of this instance" link (e.g. `/source` or in the API root) so the project's own deployment complies with §13 once anyone modifies it.
- `[ ]` **How others handle the App Store conflict.** VLC iOS: relicensed to MPL-2.0 + GPLv2+ with a repo rule that "Any commit to this repository implicitely allows VideoLAN to relicense"; "The MPLv2 is applicable for distribution on the App Store" (https://github.com/videolan/vlc-ios/blob/master/COPYING, https://www.videolan.org/press/ios2out.html). Signal iOS: plain AGPLv3 today, CLA required of contributors, so Signal as rights holder can ship under any terms (https://github.com/signalapp/Signal-iOS/blob/main/LICENSE, https://github.com/signalapp/Signal-iOS/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md); its historical GPLv3 "Additional Permissions For Submission to Apple App Store" granted conveyance "through the Apple App Store non-source executable versions... only under the Mozilla Public License version 2.0" `[!]` (text from ScanCode LicenseDB https://scancode-licensedb.aboutcode.org/signal-gpl-3.0-exception.LICENSE, not located in Signal's git history). Element: AGPLv3 for servers and now Element X iOS, with a CLA whose "sole reason... is to allow Element to dual-license" (https://element.io/blog/element-to-adopt-agplv3/, https://github.com/element-hq/element-x-ios/blob/develop/LICENSE); no App Store exception. Nextcloud iOS: GPLv3 plus a covenant in COPYING.iOS (verified verbatim): "we have committed not to pursue any license violation that results solely from the conflict between the GNU GPLv3 or any later version and the Apple App Store terms of service... as long as you comply with the GPL in all other respects, including its requirements to provide users with source code and the text of the license" (https://github.com/nextcloud/ios/blob/master/COPYING.iOS); Nextcloud has no CLA, so this is a contributors' covenant rather than a formal §7 permission.
- `[ ]` **Mechanism available to ScooterPlanner: a §7 additional permission.** AGPL §7: "'Additional permissions' are terms that supplement the terms of this License by making exceptions from one or more of its conditions... You may place additional permissions on material, added by you to a covered work, for which you have or can give appropriate copyright permission"; downstream may remove them. GNU FAQ: "If you are the copyright holder for the code, you can release it under various different non-exclusive licenses at various times" (https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#ReleaseUnderGPLAndNF). Implication: while the project has a single copyright holder this is trivial; the moment outside contributions arrive, either a CLA (Signal/Element model) or at minimum a DCO plus a CONTRIBUTING statement that contributions are licensed including the exception is needed, else fall back to the Nextcloud covenant.
- `[ ]` **Google Play is less of a problem.** The Developer Distribution Agreement grants users "a nonexclusive, worldwide, and perpetual license to... use the Product", allows the developer's own EULA (superseded only where it conflicts), and lets users reinstall "an unlimited number of times"; no noncommercial or device-count rules. Source: https://play.google/intl/en_us/developer-distribution-agreement.html (§3.9, §5.3). Implication: cover Play in the same exception for symmetry, but it is not strictly needed.
- `[ ]` **Corresponding source and licence text reachable from the app.** GPL/AGPL §6 obligations still apply to the binary: About screen links to the exact tagged source for the shipped version and shows the licence; store listing links to the repository. Implication: tag every store release; the CI (EAS) build must embed the commit hash.
- `[~]` **Recommendation.** Keep AGPL-3.0. Add to `LICENSE` (or `LICENSE-EXCEPTIONS.md` referenced from it) an "Additional permission under GNU AGPL version 3 section 7" allowing conveyance of executable versions through the Apple App Store and Google Play provided Corresponding Source remains available, modelled on Signal's text, plus a Nextcloud-style non-enforcement sentence. Record as an ADR (hard to reverse once contributors exist) and settle the CLA-vs-DCO question in the same ADR.

## Open questions surfaced

These are not answered here and are candidates for new tickets or for the grilling tickets named:

1. **Consent architecture and ad-network swap (#11):** UMP (CMP ID 300) is the simplest certified CMP, but it is Google's; an Ad Alliance network or a web tag may need a different certified CMP and the TC string must be shared across the ad-slot abstraction. Decide whether the abstraction owns consent or delegates it to the network adapter.
2. **ATT or no ATT on iOS (#11):** choosing never to request ATT yields a cleaner privacy label ("no tracking") and a no-tracking story, at the cost of personalised-ad revenue on iOS. Needs a revenue estimate.
3. **Anonymous-account deletion UX (#10):** deletion of a never-linked device account is in-app only; decide whether to show the device-account id in Settings, how backups (nightly dumps) honour deletion, and the stated retention window for the privacy policy.
4. **Web deletion page and privacy policy as deliverables:** a public `/delete-account` page (Google requires it) and an NL+EN privacy policy page need an owner and a place in the web planner; not currently in any ticket.
5. **Licence exception ADR (#14):** §7 additional permission text, CLA vs DCO, and the backend `/source` link for AGPL §13.
6. **Navigation disclaimer copy and Terms of Use:** NL + EN wording, where the Terms live, and whether the "report wrong road type" affordance is v1 (#9/#13).
7. **Expo capability check (#4):** confirm the chosen navigation/location libraries support When-In-Use background updates with `CLBackgroundActivitySession`/`allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates`, Android FGS type `location`, and a UMP wrapper, before the stack decision is final.
8. **Telemetry under ePrivacy:** crash/analytics collection either qualifies as "strikt noodzakelijk / geringe gevolgen" (no consent, must be disclosed) or needs its own purpose in the consent message; the map lists telemetry as unspecified.
9. **Unverified items to re-check before the spec relies on them:** Google's scope for credential-less accounts; honouring a web-bought entitlement on Android; Apple EU Attachment 14 commission wording; Limited ads 2.0 purpose table; Google's serving behaviour when ATT is denied; GDPR Art. 13 verbatim; RVV Bijlage 1 sign texts; Signal exception provenance.
